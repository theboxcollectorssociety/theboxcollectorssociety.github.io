Dear Box Collectors,


October was the most wonderful-fabulous month.
My partner and I got married on October 15th, and the profound sense of gratitude I have for his support and pride I have for our commitment to each other saturates my everyday.
It has been a long road for me learning how to cultivate trust in others, let alone in myself. Like the tender threads of fungi that have been bonding trees together in the darkness of the soil for over 360 million years, I feel I am slowly being woven into the fabric of the world exactly where I belong--in great part because I am finally making decisions on where to thread the needle.
I am officially 6 months post-op and my body is still healing steadily. I find comfort in my movement more days than not, which is a huge change for me. Clothes are still quite challenging. I have included a picture from our wedding below, and it is the closest I have ever come in a photo to feeling at home in my body.
I didn't own a suit until a couple of months ago. Before that, when it came to dressing up for formal occasions the only "formal" clothing I had was a dress and heels, and let me tell you it felt like wearing spiders. I didn't know how to shop for a suit on my own and was too nervous to ask for help. It also made me feel like I wanted to replace all of my clothes and that was overwhelming and expensive, so I did nothing for a long time. My closet is still changing a lot as I grow to understand what makes me feel like me on the outside.
But Hannah do clothes really matter that much? Yes. It is not about fashion or having a lot to choose from, it is about finding a few items I can put on that feel neutral to good so I can get on with my day, make art, go to work, feel capable. It is a lot like choosing my pronouns, but with pronouns it was easier because there are fewer options. When anyone uses the wrong pronouns referring to me, it is a very similar feeling to being shoved back in a dress and being told to like it. It would be very hard to put someone in a dress unwillingly once let alone multiple times without them not wanting to hang out with you anymore. Perhaps this is a helpful visual for how important it is to respect peoples' gender identities, to encourage exploration and discussion about gender in general, and ultimately create more supportive spaces for the people we know and love.


As always, thanks for reading.


xoxo,

Hannah
